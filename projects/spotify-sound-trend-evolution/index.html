Spotify Sound Trend Evolution: Centennial Audio & Lyrical Dynamics

Acoustic telemetry, lyrical sentiment dissonance, and algorithmic homogenization across 550,000 songs and 100 years

Context

This project analyzes 550,000+ Spotify tracks from 1920 to 2024 to trace music’s shift from acoustic to electric and streaming eras, asking whether lyrics grew sadder as beats got happier, sonic variety declined, and streaming shortened tracks or blurred genre boundaries.

Designed Solution

A unified audio and text analysis pipeline standardizes acoustic features, scores lyrical sentiment, and tracks annual diversity, loudness, and duration. It identifies upbeat music paired with negative lyrics and projects normalized audio features into a 2D t-SNE map to show how genre clusters have shifted and converged over time.

Experimental Process

Uncovering centennial cultural and production trends across 550,000 tracks required a structured, five-stage investigative process: sanitizing century-spanning telemetry, formalizing lyrical-musical dissonance, computing annual diversity indices, analyzing streaming economic incentives, and embedding genre manifold geometry.

  • Phase 1: Data preparation: Clean and validate the catalog. Filtered incomplete years and invalid audio values, then prepared separate acoustic and lyrics cohorts to handle missing text. Remove invalid values and partial years before measuring trends.
  • Phase 2: Mood comparison: Compare lyrics and sound. Scored lyric sentiment and compared it with acoustic valence, revealing more upbeat production paired with negative lyrics in recent decades. Measure emotional alignment between lyrics and musical mood.
  • Phase 3: Acoustic diversity: Track changes in sonic variety. Normalized six core audio features and tracked their annual spread as a measure of sonic variety. Annual feature spread shows diversity peaking in the late 1970s before contracting after 2000.
  • Phase 4: Production trends: Measure loudness and track length. Tracked rising loudness and a 33% drop in average track duration, from 4.2 minutes in the 1980s to 2.8 minutes in 2024. Relate production loudness and shorter formats to changes in listening platforms.
  • Phase 5: Genre mapping: Map genre relationships. Projected representative tracks across eight audio features into a 2D t-SNE map to compare genre clusters over time. Compare distinct historical genre clusters with the greater overlap in modern music.
