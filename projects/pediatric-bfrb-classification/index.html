Pediatric Body-Focused Repetitive Behavior (BFRB) Classification

Multimodal time-series deep learning pipeline detecting pediatric BFRBs from wearable IMU, thermal, and ToF telemetry

Context

Body-focused repetitive behaviors such as hair pulling, skin picking, and nail biting can harm children, yet diagnosis and treatment tracking often rely on incomplete caregiver recall. This project develops a real-time classifier for subtle episodes from wrist-worn wearable signals while minimizing false alarms on everyday movements like scratching, eating, or adjusting glasses.

Designed Solution

An asymmetric dual-tower model separates high-rate motion from lower-rate proximity and thermal signals. The motion tower removes gravity and learns temporal patterns with residual CNN and BiGRU attention layers, while the spatial tower encodes ToF and thermal data; a fusion head combines them, with an IMU-only fallback for sensor dropouts and test-time augmentation for robust predictions.

Experimental Process

Developing a reliable clinical classifier from noisy wearable telemetry required addressing five fundamental challenges: isolating hand kinematics from constant gravity vectors, resolving angular velocity without gimbal lock, separating repetitive tics from everyday spontaneous movements, handling sensor dropout, and preventing severe patient-memorization across small cohorts.

Iterative Data Science Process: Physics Kinematics + Multimodal Asymmetry + TTA
92.8% F1 Score · < 2.1% False Alarm Rate on Holdout Subjects
  • Phase 1: Signal audit: Find gravity and sensor gaps. Checked motion signals for gravity effects and mapped where optical distance readings dropped out during gestures. Separate device orientation from real movement; track missing readings.
  • Phase 2: Motion features: Recover clean movement signals. Used orientation quaternions to remove gravity and derive smooth angular velocity and jerk features. Isolate true motion, including fast wrist turns.
  • Phase 3: Rhythm features: Measure repetitive movement. Extracted FFT cadence and rolling motion statistics to distinguish repeated gestures from brief movements. Capture cyclic patterns and short motion bursts.
  • Phase 4: Multimodal model: Fuse streams with a fallback. Built separate motion and spatial towers, plus an IMU-only fallback for optical sensor dropouts. Keep noisy streams isolated and maintain inference during dropouts.
  • Phase 5: Generalization: Test across varied gestures. Applied Mixup, label smoothing, and test-time shifts to reduce patient overfitting and gesture timing sensitivity. Improve robustness to different movement styles and timing.
