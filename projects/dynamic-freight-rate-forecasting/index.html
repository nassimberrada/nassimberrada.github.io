Dynamic Spot Freight Rate Forecasting & Spatial Logistics Pricing

Out-of-time walk-forward gradient boosting engine predicting spot truckload rates with geodesic routing, carrier market index elasticity, and automated data quality guardrails

Context

Spot freight rates shift with regional supply, fuel costs, equipment availability, and seasonal demand, so brokers must quote quickly while balancing carrier acceptance against margin. This model predicts rates across 60,000 shipments (48,000 historical and 12,000 prospective holdout loads), with validation designed to prevent temporal leakage, preserve anomalous data signals, handle new markets, and test holiday pricing behavior.

Designed Solution

An end-to-end scikit-learn pipeline combines geospatial, calendar, and market features with an L2-regularized HistGradientBoostingRegressor to model nonlinear pricing across short and long hauls. Haversine distance and route detour ratios describe geography, calendar harmonics model seasonal demand, and missingness flags plus unknown-tolerant market encoding help preserve data quality and handle newly appearing hubs.

Experimental Process

Developing an enterprise freight rate model required navigating critical real-world domain challenges: decoupling line-haul tariffs from fixed minimum short-haul fees, sanitizing physically impossible scale measurements without data loss, anticipating out-of-sample market index deflation, and preventing severe overconfidence from random train-test splitting on time-series records.

Analytical Pipeline: Telemetry Hygiene, Geodesic Kinematics & Walk-Forward Boosting
From Raw Corrupted Telemetry to Calibrated Rate Intelligence
  • Phase 1: Exploratory Audit & Target Skewness: Auditing Rate Skew & Distance Effects. Audited 48,000 loads and found strongly right-skewed prices. Rate-per-mile fell from $3.50–$5.20 on short hauls to $1.65–$2.10 on long routes; valid high-price outliers were retained. Used MedAE alongside MAE and RMSE; nonlinear pricing favored tree models.
  • Phase 2: Telemetry Hygiene & Indicator Engineering: Flagging Invalid Weights & Missing Signals. Flagged missing or negative weights and absent market indices before imputation, preserving rows while distinguishing corrupted readings from genuine values. Retained all training rows and preserved data-quality signals.
  • Phase 3: Geodesic Kinematics & Network Topology: Modeling Routes & New Markets. Combined Haversine distance and route detour ratios with market categories. Unknown-tolerant encoding handled eight new holdout hubs using their coordinates. Spatial features captured route friction without external routing APIs.
  • Phase 4: Walk-Forward Validation & Regime Shift: Validating with Monthly Forward Folds. Used four monthly walk-forward folds to prevent future data leakage. Forward-fold MAE averaged $161.93, 16.4% lower than the distance-only baseline. Validation reflected real deployment across changing market conditions.
  • Phase 5: Benchmark Corridor Stress-Testing: Stress-Testing a Holiday Corridor. Tested a fixed Lexington-to-Fort Wayne lane across December. The model captured the pre-Christmas rate surge, from an $840–$890 baseline to a peak above $1,140. All 12,000 holdout predictions remained positive.
