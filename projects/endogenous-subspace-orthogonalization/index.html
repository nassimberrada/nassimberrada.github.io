Endogenous Subspace Orthogonalization (Ortho-LoRA)

Targeted monosemanticity and causal activation steering via attribution-conditioned low-rank adaptation

Question

Language models rely on superposition to compress web-scale knowledge into finite geometric dimensions, creating entangled, polysemantic representations that resist clean steering. While Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) attempt to untangle these activations exogenously with severe parameter and latency overhead, circumscribed downstream tasks require only a fraction of residual capacity. Can a model instead endogenously unpack its own representations on demand by rotating task features into an orthogonal basis during adaptation?

Approach

Ortho-LoRA transforms low-rank adapter channels (ΔW = BA) into an orthogonal coordinate frame via a joint attribution-conditioned Gram penalty and bottleneck sparsity. Rather than enforcing blind perpendicularity, the loss scales angular repulsion by empirical token co-activation—forcing write vectors apart only when channels fire concurrently on real task inputs. When adaptation converges, weights fold directly into the base model with zero added inference latency.

Methodology

Empirical evaluation protocol, loss optimization formulation, and layer interventions inferred directly from the research repository (~/Code/ortho-lora):

  • Architectural Paradigms & Layer Depth Sweeps · PyTorch & PEFT. Evaluated across two contrasting transformer backbones: modern Gated SwiGLU with RMSNorm (Qwen-2.5-0.5B, d=896) and classical Dense GELU with LayerNorm (Pythia-410M, d=1024). Interventions were systematically swept across Early (Layer 6, ~25%), Mid (Layer 12, ~50%), and Late (Layer 18, ~75%) residual streams to isolate semantic abstraction from surface un-embedding biases.
  • Joint Loss Formulation & Hyperparameter Sweeps · Gram Coherence. Trained using AdamW (lr=2e-4, cosine decay, weight decay 0.01) with PyTorch FP16 mixed precision on single 16GB NVIDIA T4 GPU budgets (<7 GB peak VRAM). Grid sweeps comprehensively varied rank capacity r ∈ [4, 8, 16, 32, 64] and orthogonality weight λ_ortho ∈ [0.0, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0] alongside L1 bottleneck activation sparsity λ_sparse.
  • Comparative Baselines & Matched Capacities · SAE vs LoRA vs CAA. Benchmarked head-to-head against unconstrained Standard LoRA (λ_ortho = 0.0), Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA), and an exogenous Top-K Sparse Autoencoder (k=32, 4x expansion dictionary d_sae = 3,584 trained to Fraction of Variance Unexplained FVU < 0.01) fitted directly to Layer 12 activations.
  • Downstream Tasks & Counterfactual Steerability · Evals Suite. Evaluated across affective sentiment classification (tweet_eval), continuous safety moderation (real-toxicity-prompts), and stereotypical bias (StereoSet). Steerability measured via the Steering Selectivity Index (SSI), tracking target probability change (ΔP) relative to off-target KL divergence drift (D_KL) across steering multipliers γ ∈ [-6, +6].

Findings

Experimental verification, geometric superposition collapse metrics, and theoretical boundary conditions synthesized from published paper tables and benchmark sweeps:

  • Superposition Collapse & Metric Separation (C_gram: 0.719 → 0.070). Reduced cross-talk up to 10.3x and raised effective rank capacity from 57.5% to 87.7%.
  • Causal Steering Selectivity & Zero Leakage (+41.6% vs LoRA · 13.2x vs CAA). Reached SSI 3.837, with 16.3x less collateral drift than standard LoRA.
  • Radical Parameter & Latency Efficiency vs. SAEs (112x Fewer Params). Uses 112x fewer parameters than a matched SAE and adds 0 ms latency when merged.
  • Phase Transition Boundaries & Layer Depth Limits (Capacity Threshold r ≥ 8). Rank below 8 caused capacity collapse; mid-layer interventions produced the strongest causal steering.
